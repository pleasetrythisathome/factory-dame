# Monetization: default to open-source / community-first

**Date:** 2026-05-19
**Author:** Scarlet (working stance, low conviction)
**Conviction:** notion

## Stance

Default monetization posture for factory-dame is **open-source / community-first**. The engine, voice primitives, and the `nd-*` CLIs ship under a permissive license. Commercial productization (boxed hardware, hosted demos, SaaS) is held back as a *contingent path* — pursued only if external demand materializes through unsolicited inbound from modular synth, jazz, or visual-art communities.

This is a notion, not a decision. It sits below the deeper principle that factory-dame is a creative counterweight to aswritten, where commercial pressure is precisely what we're not importing.

## Why this stance fits the existing graph

- `Claim_PiShelved` (foundation) already records "Raspberry Pi or custom SBC deployment is shelved unless external demand for a low-cost form factor materializes." The same conditional applies one level up to all commercialization.
- `Narrative_CreativeCounterweightToAswritten` (strategy) frames the project as breathing room from business velocity. A premature monetization plan would re-import the energy we're explicitly trying to keep out.
- `Claim_NoveltyConstraintOnCounterweight` (foundation) adds that novelty — not creativity alone — is the bar. A commercial product needs to clear the same novelty bar, which forecloses the easy paths (eurorack module replicating existing functionality, VCV Rack plugin, paid software synth).

## Trigger conditions for revisiting

Promote this notion to a claim — and start drafting commercial-path memories — when *any* of the following lands:

1. **Unsolicited inbound** from a person in the relevant communities (modular synth builder, jazz performer commissioning a piece, visual artist asking to license) that's specific enough to scope a paid engagement.
2. **A novel demo** that can't be replicated by any existing tool — e.g. the per-voice motor coupling phase of `task-011` running publicly on arbitrary live audio, with audience reaction strong enough to suggest paying-customer demand.
3. **Hardware path forced by performance** — if the M1 Mac mini path doesn't carry the live engine plus visualization plus CV output simultaneously, custom embedded becomes a capability question (not just a packaging one), which would shift the cost-benefit toward eventually selling the hardware.

## What this does NOT decide

- Pricing model if commercial path opens. Held open until inbound or trigger materializes.
- Whether to take outside funding. Out of scope; factory-dame is self-funded by definition of the counterweight role.
- Whether content/showcase work (e.g. an audio-visual installation, a piece performed at a jazz venue) counts as "commercialization." It doesn't — those are part of the creative artifact stream, separate from a productized engine.
